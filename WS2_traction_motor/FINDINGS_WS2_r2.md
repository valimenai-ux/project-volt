## 3. New findings

### WS2-F8 — MATERIAL. `heat_worst_case_kW = 8.75` is the V1-speed crawl; the report's own hotter V2-speed crawl rejects ~10.2 kW, so the loop-sizing interface member understates the workstream's own worst case by ~14–17%

**What is wrong.** The assignment's crawl case is "510 Nm at 10.6–23.6 km/h" — a pair, and WS2's §3 table dutifully computes both: V1 (760 rpm) 146.1 °C steady, V2 (1,692 rpm) **150.7 °C steady — the hotter of the two**. But `run_ws2.py` computes `crawl_loss_kW` only at `REQ["crawl_v_kmh"][0]` (10.6 km/h), and that 8.75 kW is what §8, the ledger CSV, and `interface.coolant.heat_worst_case_kW` ("size the LT loop to this") all export. Re-derived at the V2 crawl point under the identical bookkeeping convention (510 Nm, 1,692 rpm, 370 V, T_wind = 170 °C): copper 6.91 + iron 2.20 + windage 0.06 + inverter 1.05 = **10.22 kW** — the iron member grows 0.90 → 2.20 kW with electrical frequency and the (ψs/ψref)² ≈ 7.2 flux factor. At the actual 150.7 °C steady winding the loop heat is ~9.9 kW (machine 8.84 + inverter 1.05 — which back-solves WS2's own published 150.7 °C exactly: Ts = 65 + 8,840/700 = 77.6; Tw = 77.6 + 6,580/90 = 150.7). Either way the true crawl worst case exceeds the exported 8.75 kW. This is precisely the round-1 F2 class repeated one level up: the field is now named correctly but holds the milder member of the set the report itself describes.

**Why it matters.** WS6 sizes the LT loop from this member; the ledger row is at least honestly labeled "(V1 speed)", but the interface `worst_case` string is not qualified, and a consumer reading the block alone under-sizes by ~1.5 kW at the single duty point the whole cooling argument (WS2-E1) hangs on.

**Resolution.** Compute the crawl loss at both crawl speeds and export the max (V2, ~10.2 kW at the 170 °C convention) as `heat_worst_case_kW`, or add the V2 member alongside a relabeled V1 field; propagate to §8 prose and the ledger row. Note the dependency on WS2-E1: if the lead descopes sustained crawl to a 1-min case, the loop-sizing case changes entirely and this field must be re-derived, not just relabeled.

### WS2-F9 — minor. §2.2's corrected paragraph still contains two claims contradicted by its own table

The follow-on sentence: "would need a rotor/bearing requalification to 7,900–8,600 rpm for a **≤1.3 kg/ratio-point** mass saving and <2% loss saving." The sweep's own mass column gives 7.1 kg (10:1→11:1) and 5.9 kg (11:1→12:1) per ratio point — ~5× the stated figure (possibly a slipped decimal from the 13.0 kg total at 12:1). And "the only **material** loss gains (11:1, 12:1)": 11:1's total-loss gain is 0.15% (4.022 vs 4.028 kWh) — *below* the 0.17% the same paragraph dismisses as noise-level for 9:1; only 12:1 (2.0%) is material. Both errors point in the retained decision's favour; the decision itself stands on the rpm-line breach and the >5% bar regardless. Fix the two clauses.

### WS2-F10 — minor. HV cable sizing currents use nominal bus voltage while the same report sizes the motor cable to a window-floor bounding envelope

`bus.trade.400V_class` sizes the genset feed at 338 A (125 kW / **370 V**) on 150 mm² (ampacity 370 A) and the pack run at 162 A (v_nom). But sustained charge-depleted operation puts the bus near the string floor: 125 kW at 300–336 V is 372–417 A — at or above the chosen cable's ampacity — and the report itself quotes pack current 400 A *at the floor* two lines later. The motor cable, by contrast, gets a deliberate 533 A floor-voltage bounding envelope. The asymmetry survived two rounds. No conclusion changes (a size-up strengthens the already-decisive 11.2 kg argument for 400 V class), but the sizing convention should be voltage-consistent before WS6 inherits the cable table. Carried over from round 1, not introduced by the rework.
